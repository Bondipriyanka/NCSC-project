# 🎤 NCSC 2026: 10-MINUTE PRESENTATION SCRIPT & Q&A GUIDE

In NCSC, you get **strictly 10 minutes** to present. If you go over time, the judges will stop you and deduct points. You should practice with a stopwatch! 

Here is a simple, high-scoring script split between **Student 1 (Group Leader)** and **Student 2 (Group Member)**.

---

## ⏱️ Presentation Timeline (10 Minutes Total)
*   **Minutes 0:00 - 2:00 (2 mins):** Introduction, Local Problem & Objectives (Student 1)
*   **Minutes 2:00 - 5:00 (3 mins):** Review of Literature & Methodology (Student 2)
*   **Minutes 5:00 - 8:00 (3 mins):** Data Observations, Accuracy Analysis & App Demo (Student 1 & 2)
*   **Minutes 8:00 - 10:00 (2 mins):** Conclusion, Future Scope & Suggestions (Student 2)

---

## 🗣️ The Script

### 🎬 Part 1: The Hook & The Problem (Minutes 0:00 - 2:00)
**Student 1:**
> "Respectable judges, teachers, and friends. Good morning/afternoon. I am [Your Name], the group leader, and this is my partner [Teammate's Name]. We represent [School Name], [District Name] of [State Name].
> 
> Our project is titled **'Smart Fruits & Vegetables Freshness Detection App: A Local Solution to Reduce Post-Harvest Food Waste Using Mobile AI'**.
> 
> Every day while walking to school, we noticed street vendors discarding large amounts of blackening bananas and soft tomatoes into the open drains of our neighborhood. When we spoke to them, we learned they lose around 5 to 8 kilograms of produce every week. This isn't just an environmental hazard—it is a direct financial loss to our poorest community members.
> 
> Under the theme *Science for Sustainable Development*, our project aims to solve this local issue by introducing a simple, free, image-based AI mobile tool to classify fruit freshness and suggest quick actions to prevent waste.
> 
> Our main objectives were to:
> 1. Survey the local vendors to calculate waste.
> 2. Create a simple mobile classifier using Google Teachable Machine and MIT App Inventor.
> 3. Test its accuracy in real-world market stalls."

---

### 🧪 Part 2: Review of Literature & Methodology (Minutes 2:00 - 5:00)
**Student 2:**
> "Thank you, [Student 1]. Before building our solution, we studied the science behind food spoilage. As fruits ripen, they release ethylene gas, converting starches to sugars and causing cellular walls to soften. If left unchecked, microbial mold and bacteria set in, making food unsafe.
> 
> According to the UN Food Waste Index, consumers waste millions of tons of food. While laboratories use expensive gas sensors or spectrometers to check freshness, our local sellers cannot afford such tools.
> 
> This led us to develop our project using a **scientific methodology**:
> *   First, we conducted a field survey of 10 local vendors and 15 households in our area.
> *   Second, we captured over 100 photos of local tomatoes and bananas at different stages of decay.
> *   Third, we uploaded these photos to Google Teachable Machine to train our machine learning model into 5 color-coded categories: Good (Green), Moderately Fresh (Yellow), Ripened (Orange), Over-Ripened (Purple), and Rotten (Red).
> *   Fourth, we integrated this AI model into an Android app using MIT App Inventor. The app uses the phone’s camera, displays the freshness level, and suggests actions."

---

### 📊 Part 3: Data, Results & App Demo (Minutes 5:00 - 8:00)
**Student 1:**
> "Let look at our data. Our survey showed that our local vendors throw away an average of **5.8 kg of fresh produce weekly**, leading to losses of up to ₹500 a week. 
> 
> Additionally, we found that 40% of households identify freshness purely by eye, which leads to early discard of slightly spotted, yet safe, fruits.
> 
> We tested our finished application on 30 fresh and decaying fruits in our local shops. The app correctly identified the condition of **26 out of 30 fruits**, giving us a scientific accuracy of **86.6%**. 
> 
> Let us show you a quick live demonstration. *(Student 1 holds up a phone, takes a photo of a ripe banana brought for presentation, and shows the screen).*
> 
> As you can see, the app immediately identifies this banana as **'Ripened (Orange)'** and recommends consuming it today. If we scanned an over-ripe spotted banana, it would show **'Over-Ripened (Purple)'** and suggest using it for juices or smoothies immediately."

**Student 2:**
> "During our tests, we also encountered some errors. In poor light or under direct sun reflections, the camera struggled, misclassifying 4 items. This showed us that while the AI is highly useful, camera quality and lighting are important factors we must improve."

---

### 🏁 Part 4: Conclusion & Recommendations (Minutes 8:00 - 10:00)
**Student 2:**
> "To conclude, our project shows that children can utilize simple, free technological tools like AI to solve local community issues. 
> 
> We recommend the following to our local community:
> 1.  **Dynamic Pricing:** Vendors should sell 'Orange' and 'Purple' category fruits at 50% discount to ensure they are eaten rather than wasted.
> 2.  **Organic Composting:** 'Red (Rotten)' fruits should be separated early to prevent spoiling neighboring fruits, and sent to municipal compost bins to make fertilizer.
> 3.  **Dynamic Recipes:** Households should check the color code and use over-ripe fruits for jams, purees, or baking rather than throwing them away.
> 
> By taking these simple steps, our neighborhood can significantly reduce carbon emissions from rotting landfills, protect local economic livelihoods, and support sustainable development.
> 
> We would like to thank our guide teacher, our school computer lab, and the local vendors of [Market Name] who helped us collect data. Thank you. We are now open to any questions you may have."

## 🙋 Common Q&A (How to Impress the Judges)

NCSC judges want to ensure the **students** did the work, not their parents or coaching centers. Use these guidelines to answer:

### Q1: What was YOUR role, and what did your teacher/parent do?
*   **Bad Answer:** "My teacher built the app and my dad wrote the report."
*   **Excellent Answer:** "Our guide teacher helped us understand the scientific method and set our objectives. Our parents helped us stay safe during market visits. However, **we did all the field surveys ourselves, took all the photographs, trained the AI model in our computer lab, and wrote this report.**"

### Q2: Why did you choose Google Teachable Machine and MIT App Inventor instead of professional code?
*   **Answer:** "As 10th-grade students, our focus was on creating a **cost-effective and rapid prototype** that any student can copy. These tools are free, require zero subscription costs, and run easily on basic Android devices, which aligns with NCSC's focus on simple, affordable, local innovations."

### Q3: What were the main errors you faced in the app and how did you resolve them?
*   **Answer:** "The biggest error was **lighting and glare**. A shiny tomato under a bright market bulb reflected light, making the camera think it had white rot spots. We solved this by teaching vendors to take photos in indirect shade or by holding the phone slightly angled to avoid direct reflections."

### Q4: How is this related to 'Sustainable Development'?
*   **Answer:** "This project directly addresses **SDG 12: Responsible Consumption and Production**. By classifying food freshness, we prevent premature disposal, reduce the load on local waste dumps (which produce methane gas), and improve the economic sustainability of small street vendors."

### Q5: How can a local vendor who cannot read or write use this app?
*   **Answer:** "That is why we designed a **traffic-light color code**. Even if a vendor cannot read the screen, seeing a large **🟢 (Green) or 🔴 (Red)** light makes it immediately clear what action to take. We kept textual menus to a absolute minimum."
